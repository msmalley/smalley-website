Your posting describes building the machine that builds the feature. That is the work I have been doing for the past year, and I wrote about it publicly in July in an article called "Building the Machine That Builds Your Career". At Moddable I run a four-repo game engine where autonomous research and implementation agents work the issue backlog: they read the spec, open the pull request, and run the tests. In the twelve days to 10 September that pipeline produced 207 commits, took playable game variants from 198 to 230, and grew the engine suite to 6,887 tests. I wrote very few of those commits myself. I designed the system that produces them, the tool surface it calls, and the review gates that make its output trustworthy.

The engineering underneath that is first-principles systems work, not prompt wrangling. One example: supporting Alice Chess, where every piece moves to the matching square on a second board, could have been a special case. Instead I made cell adjacency declarative so that a stack of boards is one board with a layer coordinate, which unblocked six multi-board variants at once instead of one. I also own the product surface: 89 AI-callable tools over MCP, REST and OpenAPI, so an agent can drive the engine directly. Before this I spent 15 years shipping into regulated financial infrastructure, custody, KYC/AML and licensed fintech, and led teams of up to 30 while still writing production code. I work fully remote from the UK and would welcome a conversation.
